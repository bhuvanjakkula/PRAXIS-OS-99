# PFM protected-spending review

Integrated 4 October 2026 from supplied research leads. Local statistical associations in the summary are not imported as general effect sizes. Its narrative and figures are not reproduced.

The IMF evidence workflow now reviews commitment controls, treasury cash management, ledger reconciliation and exception resolution. Any PFM note enables all four checks plus linked budget-execution, facility-verification and authority evidence. Notes capture appropriation codes and overrides, TSA coverage and cash-release bottlenecks, unmatched records and arrears, and owned exception closure with service-continuity safeguards.

A treasury single account supports unified cash visibility and management; it does not by itself ring-fence sector funds or guarantee timely releases. Accounting reconciliation and commitment controls depend on coverage, implementation and applicable authority. Automated software does not eliminate off-budget activity or establish verified real-time auditing merely by recording a note.

Primary context: [IMF commitment-control guidance](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/tnm/issues/2016/12/31/commitment-controls-23302) discusses expenditure controls, arrears and implementation preconditions. [IMF TSA research](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2016/12/31/treasury-single-account-concept-design-and-implementation-issues-23927) describes cash consolidation and management. These publications support the control areas, not a claim that this prototype enforces them.

This implementation stores review evidence and flags gaps. It has no treasury/bank connection and performs no payment, transaction suspension, account sweep or legal sanction. Actual controls require authorized systems, segregation of duties, reliable records, exception procedures and independent verification. The existing nominal/real spending comparator remains an analytical tool rather than an adopted budget rule.
