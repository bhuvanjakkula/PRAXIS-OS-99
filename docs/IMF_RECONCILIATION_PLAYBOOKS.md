# Reconciliation exception-playbook review

Integrated 5 October 2026 from supplied research leads. Adds rule/version review, matching/tolerance review and recovery escalation. Any playbook note enables all three reviews plus links to retry safety, settlement status and exception closure evidence.

Record approved exception taxonomy, scope, version, tests and change authority. Matching review must distinguish deterministic identifiers from ambiguous fuzzy candidates and document currency, fee and partial-payment semantics, approved tolerances and false-match evaluation. Recovery review must document grace windows, unknown outcomes, quarantine, reviewer deadlines, compensation limits and verified closure.

Fuzzy similarity is not proof that two financial records describe the same obligation. An amount inside a tolerance does not itself authorize a write-off or ledger adjustment. A grace window may postpone escalation but must not hide a genuine break. A compensating application action may not reverse an irrevocable bank transfer. An AI research manifesto or reported case-study savings is not evidence of safe autonomous recovery in a different financial system.

The supplied percentage improvements, automatic-guarantee claims and cross-domain algorithm claims remain unverified research leads. No universal benefit or parameter-free break guarantee is adopted. Original narrative and figures are not reproduced.

This release stores evidence and flags gaps. It implements no fuzzy matcher, process-mining engine, RPA, adaptive agent, ledger adjustment or financial reversal. Automated recovery remains unauthorized. Existing retry review relies on provider-specific semantics rather than assuming exactly-once external execution.
Primary engineering reference: Microsoft compensating-transaction guidance (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/compensating-transaction) describes application-specific recovery, idempotent steps and possible manual intervention. These are design constraints, not a guarantee of bank-transfer reversibility.
