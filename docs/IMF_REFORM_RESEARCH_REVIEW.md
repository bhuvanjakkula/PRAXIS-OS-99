# IMF reform research integration

Reviewed 4 October 2026. The supplied research summary is treated as leads, not an independently verified consensus. Its prose/figures are not reproduced in this release.

## Evidence and corrections

Kentikelenis and Stubbs' [2024 research paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12992-024-01045-9) examined 21 programs signed in 2020–2022, with reviews through August 2023. It reported reduced fiscal space in 15 countries, inflation eroding gains, and implementation gaps in spending floors. These findings concern a defined historical sample; they do not establish the performance of every current program. A nominal floor is formally a minimum; spending close to a floor does not make it a contractual ceiling.

The pasted claim of only two Sub-Saharan African Board chairs is outdated. The [IMF's November 2024 announcement](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2024/11/01/pr-24403-imf-expands-executive-board-with-addition-of-25th-chair) records three chairs and 45 member countries reorganized into three constituencies. Continental country counts should not be substituted for IMF constituency membership.

Other pasted quota percentages and GDP parity claims are not carried into the product as verified current facts. Governance proposals must reference the [dated IMF quota and voting table](https://www.imf.org/en/about/executive-board/members-quotas) and distinguish quota from voting share and purchasing-power from market-exchange-rate GDP. Historical literature should be cited by original publication date rather than a later repository upload date.

## Added technology

IMF Solutions now records governance, conditionality and operational-independence review notes. These capture proposed reform authority, country ownership, macroeconomic rationale, alternatives, conflicts and accountability without determining legal authority or policy effectiveness.

An optional social-spending comparison accepts baseline spending, current actual spending, current nominal floor, matching-period price indices, scope, currency/unit, source and comparability evidence. It computes actual spending at baseline prices as `actual * baseline_index / current_index`, then change relative to baseline spending. Floor attainment is reported separately. Missing actuals remain missing; zero is a valid measurement. Nonpositive baseline spending/indices and negative spending are rejected.

For example, spending rising from 100 to 110 with the price index rising from 100 to 125 becomes 88 at baseline prices, a 12% decline, despite meeting a nominal floor of 105. This is arithmetic on supplied evidence, not causal attribution, a recommendation to change fiscal policy, a per-capita comparison or proof that beneficiaries are protected. Reviewers must ensure comparable periods, price-index bases, coverage and currency units; CPI may be an imperfect public-service cost deflator.

## Further proposals

Governance scenario comparison, permissioned debt coordination and capital-flow stress/insurance evaluation remain future pilots. They require dated authoritative inputs, country participation, independent evaluation and financial/legal review before operational use. Software cannot itself reallocate IMF voting power or create creditor agreement.
