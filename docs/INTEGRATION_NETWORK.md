# Collaboration networks and aviation coordination — 2026-10-03

## CTO collaboration and knowledge transfer

Open `/#cto`, select a decision and enable CTO execution review, then enable
collaboration network review. Supply 2–40 organizational teams or partners and
up to 200 unique undirected relationships. Each relationship records whether it
is observed or proposed, its evidence date, reference, owner and learning outcome.
Observed links without evidence or beyond the supplied age limit are excluded.

The current network reports degree, normalized degree and betweenness centrality,
local clustering, connected groups, density, cross-function connections and
internal/external connections. Removing a unit is a structural sensitivity test:
it counts newly disconnected pairs among the remaining units. It does not score
an individual's performance, influence or employment value.

Proposed relationships appear only in a separate hypothetical graph. Numbered
nodes correspond to the metric table. External partners use a different color;
proposals use dashed lines. No optimum centrality, patent increase or causal
innovation benefit is inferred. Missing relationships and scope boundaries can
materially change the results. The synthetic example is explicitly illustrative.

## Cross-functional handoffs

The CTO network and aviation coordination editor share an optional workflow:
captured source → shared interpretation and acceptance → bounded trial → documented
result review. Each handoff has sending/receiving teams, an owner, due date and
evidence. A claimed reviewed stage without result evidence remains incomplete.
Negative and inconclusive results are valid observations. Overdue incomplete
handoffs identify a supplied escalation owner; no messages are sent automatically.

## Aviation organizational review

Open `/#aircrew`, select a decision, and enable aviation coordination review in
the incident record form. Review seven organizational areas: fatigue/rostering
interfaces; cross-team communication; approved support routes; environmental and
ergonomic reporting; controlled procedures; audit follow-up; and reporting channels.
Missing owners, references, unknown statuses and overdue review dates produce
explicit follow-up actions. All pass declarations remain unverified.

Use de-identified organizational references. Personal clinical records belong in
authorized systems, not these shared planning records. This feature does not
diagnose illness, assess personal fitness, optimize rosters, calculate fatigue
scores, determine duty-time legality, certify SMS/FRMS compliance or issue flight
clearance. Actual operational decisions remain with authorized people and approved
processes. Existing cockpit replay remains an offline hypothetical simulation.

The organizational structure was checked against the FAA's overview of
[SMS components](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/sms/explained/components)
and ICAO's [flight operations and fatigue-management guidance](https://www.icao.int/operational-safety/flight-ops).
These sources support organizational risk management, assurance, communication
and operator oversight; they do not validate this software or its effectiveness.

## Data and provenance

The supplied CTO network, cross-functional integration, knowledge absorption and
aircrew research summaries informed the feature themes. Duplicate attachments were
consolidated. Their study-specific estimates were not adopted as coefficients and
the complete summaries are not redistributed. No live flight, personnel, medical,
FOQA, LOSA or private research feed is connected.

Records use the existing revision-bound CTO and incident endpoints, tenant/role
controls and JSON exports. No database migration or overwrite is required.
