# Protected-sector program design review

Integrated 4 October 2026. The supplied literature summary is treated as research leads; quantitative claims and universal sector effects are not adopted as product assumptions.

The IMF workflow now includes structural benchmarks alongside proposed targets, indicative targets, quantitative performance criteria and prior actions. Optional program-design notes cover social-floor alignment with fiscal/borrowing/wage ceilings and adjustors; dated target/review/waiver history; subsidy/tariff compensation and household effects; and committed versus assumed external financing with shortfall scenarios. Any design note enables review of all four areas and linked authority/waiver evidence. Recorded classification does not establish protection or authorize lending decisions.

The [IMF conditionality factsheet](https://www.imf.org/en/about/factsheets/sheets/2023/imf-conditionality) explains that missed structural benchmarks and indicative targets do not require waivers but enter overall program review. They should not be described as irrelevant to financing decisions. A missed quantitative performance criterion can receive a Board-approved waiver. Prior actions concern prerequisites; completing an action does not guarantee sustained budget delivery.

The [2024 program-design guidance](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/007/2024/004/article-A001-en.xml) and [2025 expenditure-conditionality research](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/01/23/designing-expenditure-policy-conditionality-in-imf-supported-programs-561194) motivate examining definitions, implementation capacity and country-specific trade-offs. External aid or investment should not be assumed to materialize merely because a benchmark is met.

This release records evidence and flags gaps. It does not solve a macroeconomic financing model, negotiate ceilings, verify donor commitments or establish causal effects. Reviewers must reconcile dates, scopes and program documents before assessing protected-budget realization.
